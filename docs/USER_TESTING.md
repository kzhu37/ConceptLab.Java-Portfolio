# User Testing and Adoption

ConceptLab was not developed only as a private coding exercise. During development, I shared it with **60+ users and testers**, including classmates, friends, and peers, so I could see how the product behaved outside my own workflow.

Several of those students went beyond a one-time test and began using ConceptLab to support their own studying.

## What the 60+ figure means

I use the wording **"60+ users and testers"** deliberately.

ConceptLab did not include production analytics that measured monthly active users, retention, session counts, or learning-outcome percentages. I therefore do not claim those metrics.

The figure represents people who used or tested the project during its development and iteration. The strongest qualitative outcome was that some classmates and friends chose to continue using it for their own learning after being introduced to it.

## How testing influenced the project

Putting ConceptLab in front of other students reinforced a shift in how I thought about building software.

The project began with an instinct to keep adding functionality. User-facing development made a different question more important: **does this feature make studying clearer, more useful, or more effective?**

That change in perspective is reflected in several later product decisions:

- prioritizing application-oriented practice rather than only definition recall;
- making quiz generation configurable rather than forcing one fixed format;
- adding seen-question and answer-uniqueness safeguards to reduce repetitive practice;
- connecting feedback to relevant resources and flashcards;
- simplifying and stabilizing the desktop application after earlier UI-framework friction;
- hardening the AI pipeline around actual failure modes instead of assuming model output would always be usable.

## Evidence standard for this portfolio

This public portfolio intentionally distinguishes between facts I can support and metrics I cannot.

### Claims I make

- ConceptLab was independently designed and developed by Kevin Zhu.
- It was used or tested by 60+ users/testers during development.
- Several classmates, friends, and peers adopted it to support their own studying.

### Claims I do not make

- 60+ monthly active users;
- a specific retention rate;
- a quantified increase in grades or test scores;
- a specific number of generated study sessions;
- statistically measured learning outcomes.

Those would require telemetry or a controlled evaluation that the project did not collect.

## Why this mattered to me

The value of the testing was not the largest possible user count. It was the transition from building something that worked for me to building something that other people could understand and choose to use.

That experience made ConceptLab as much a product-design and iteration project as a programming project.
